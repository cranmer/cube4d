MagicCube4D

A four-dimensional Rubik’s cube, in your browser.

This is a modern web port, by Kyle Cranmer and Claude, of the original MagicCube4D.

Don Hatch and Melinda Green developed that puzzle on and off starting in 1988. Jay Berkenbilt and Roice Nelson later joined and made major contributions. Theirs is the work that matters here: the n-dimensional geometry engine underneath, which constructs each puzzle from a Schläfli symbol rather than hard-coding it, is Don Hatch’s.

Every one of the 128 puzzles that engine can build is here, and solve files from the original open and replay unchanged.

Classic

The full catalog — all 128 puzzles, free rotation, save and load, and the Hall of Fame records replaying at your own pace. Closest to the original.

Puzzle gallery

Every puzzle worth solving, pictured — from the familiar 3×3×3×3 to a 120-cell made of dodecahedra. Pick one and it opens in the classic app.

Multi-view

The same puzzle from up to three angles at once. Twist in any pane and every pane turns; hover a sticker and it lights up in all of them.

3D cube

An ordinary Rubik’s cube — 2×2×2 up to 7×7×7, and a megaminx — on the same engine, for learning this interface on a shape you can already picture before adding a fourth dimension.

Hall of Fame

Real solves people emailed to Superliminal, some of them records. Each opens at the position its solver faced — press Play to watch it come apart.
